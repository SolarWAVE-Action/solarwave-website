California Local Energy Investment Framework

Large New Electricity Loads Should Pay Their Full Grid Costs — and Help Build the Local Clean Energy That Protects Ratepayers.

The Strategic Opportunity

California is entering a period of major electricity-demand growth. Data centers and other large new loads are colliding with an affordability crisis — creating public backlash, but also a rare policy opening. Our proposition: large new loads should pay the full incremental costs they create, and help build the local solar, storage, and flexible-demand resources that make the grid — and the communities hosting that growth — stronger. This isn't a subsidy for solar or an unlimited surcharge on growth; it's a grid-planning and cost- responsibility framework tied to measurable impact.

A near-term legislative opening gives us an immediate way into the current Sacramento discussion. Our longer-term objective is a durable California Local Energy Investment Framework that applies to qualifying large new loads statewide, advanced through 2027–2028.

Our Approach

  • 01 Build the technical, legal, and policy foundation — an independent cost-causation analysis, policy principles, and a discussion draft — that turns SolarWAVE's vision into a credible, fundable program of work.
  • 02 Advance the policy through a three-vehicle strategy — legislation, CPUC and agency advocacy, and budget and funding — paired with local demonstration projects, so momentum continues even if any single path stalls.

01 Track

Evidence Foundation

"Cost Causation, Not Cost Shifting" — An Independent Analysis of Large-Load Grid Costs and the Path to Affordable Local Clean Energy

This foundation phase builds the evidentiary record that regulators, legislators, and opponents will test. It will:

  • Quantify the transmission, distribution, generation, and procurement costs created by large-load growth, and how those costs are allocated to ratepayers today
  • Build an independent, open-methods analysis comparing conventional grid expansion to portfolios incorporating local solar, storage, demand response, and flexible load
  • Define the legal nexus between large-load cost responsibility and measurable local benefit, including a narrowly scoped regulatory safe harbor for qualifying local projects
  • Assess ownership, financing, and interconnection barriers to determine what makes local clean-energy projects genuinely bankable
  • Provides a 25-year cost model comparing solar-owning and non-solar households, incorporating rate trajectories, equipment costs, and available financing structures
  • Publish methods, assumptions, sensitivity cases, and data transparently for independent review
02 Track

Policy Action

Once the foundation is in place, we advance the policy through these vehicles that launch together and run in parallel through 2027:

  • This session's legislative opening (near-term) — Track the most relevant bill through the close of session; preserve the strongest large-load responsibility concepts as the opening move, not the end point.
  • California Local Energy Investment Framework (primary legislation, 2027) — Statutory framework requiring qualifying large loads to pay full incremental grid costs and invest in local solar, storage, and community ownership. Introduction gated on a December 2026 readiness review, with a narrower study or pilot bill as fallback.
  • CPUC & agency advocacy (ongoing) — Parallel advocacy at the CPUC, CEC, CAISO, and with CCAs and municipal utilities on tariffs, avoided-cost methodology, locational value, interconnection, and large-load rate design.
  • Build a Sacramento power map, agency map, and opposition map to ground the strategy in real relationships and data
